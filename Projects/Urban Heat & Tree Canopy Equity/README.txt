# Philadelphia Urban Heat Island & Tree Canopy Spatial Analysis

An empirical spatial assessment evaluating microclimate vulnerability, tree canopy deficit, and thermal risk priority clusters across census tracts in Philadelphia, PA[cite 1, 2, 3].

---

## 📊 Analytical Breakdown & Key Findings

### 1. Citywide Temperature Distribution (Figure 1)
 Thermal Range Mean Land Surface Temperature (LST) ranges from $80^circtext{F}$ to $115^circtext{F}$ across Philadelphia census tracts.
 Thermal Core Temperatures above $105^circtext{F}$–$115^circtext{F}$ are densely concentrated across North Philadelphia, Lower Northeast, and central industrial corridors[cite 1].
 Cooling Corridors Temperatures below $90^circtext{F}$ are strictly confined to major contiguous green infrastructure, notably Wissahickon Valley Park, Pennypack Park, and the Fairmount Park system[cite 1].

### 2. Bivariate Vulnerability Matrix (Figure 2)
The 3x3 bivariate classification evaluates the direct relationship between LST and tree canopy percentage across all census tracts

 Critical Risk Zone (High Heat  Low Canopy) 24.0% of Philadelphia census tracts fall into the highest vulnerability classification (dark red), forming a broad, contiguous band across North and South Philadelphia[cite 2].
 High Canopy Refuges (Low Heat  High Canopy) 26.6% of tracts represent urban cool refuges (dark green), heavily concentrated in Northwest Philadelphia and the Far Northeast[cite 2].
 Moderate Conditions The remaining tracts exhibit intermediate thermalcanopy relationships, including Moderate Heat  Low Canopy (19.3%) and Moderate Heat  Moderate Canopy (9.4%)[cite 2].

### 3. Spatial Autocorrelation & Priority Clusters (Figure 3)
Using Getis-Ord $G_i^$ spatial statistics ($1.2text{ km}$ fixed distance band), the analysis isolates statistically significant neighborhood-scale clustering from baseline urban noise

 Statistically Significant Hot Spots ($90%text{--}95%text{ Confidence}$) Significant hot spot clusters ($Z  1.65$) emerge directly within North Philadelphia (centered near Hunting ParkKensington) and isolated South Philadelphia industrialcommercial tracts[cite 3]. These represent high-priority targets where low canopy directly couples with regional heat aggregation[cite 3].
 Statistically Significant Cold Spots ($90%text{--}99%text{ Confidence}$) Cold spot clusters ($Z  -1.65$) map closely to regional parklands, including the Wissahickon Creek corridor, Pennypack Park, and the John Heinz National Wildlife Refuge area in the Southwest[cite 3].
 Baseline Conditions (Not Significant) The vast majority of census tracts display no statistically significant spatial autocorrelation, confirming that severe thermal vulnerability is concentrated in specific, actionable urban corridors rather than uniformly spread citywide[cite 3].
