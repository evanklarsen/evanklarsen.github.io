Philadelphia Environmental Justice & Climate Risk Index

A census-tract-level composite index across 408 tracts
QGIS Python / GeoPandas qgis2web / Leaflet EPA EJScreen CDC PLACES Census ACS

Where you live in Philadelphia meaningfully shapes the environmental and health risks you're exposed to day to day, air quality, industrial proximity, tree canopy, and economic opportunity all vary sharply by neighborhood, and rarely in isolation. Communities vulnerable to one risk are frequently vulnerable to several at once.

This project builds a single composite score for every census tract in Philadelphia, combining environmental burden data, community health outcomes, social vulnerability, and land-cover-derived climate exposure, modeled on the methodology behind EPA's EJScreen tool. Everything below is built from public data using open-source tools, code and full methodology linked at the bottom of this page.

Breaking It Down

Four sub-indices, one composite score

Each tract's composite score is an equal-weighted average of four sub-indices, each built from a different data source and normalized to a 0–100 percentile scale.

Environmental Burden

PM2.5, ozone, diesel particulate matter, air toxics cancer risk, and traffic proximity, sourced from EPA's EJScreen dataset. Higher scores reflect greater exposure to pollution from industrial activity, vehicle traffic, and land use.

Health Vulnerability

Community-level prevalence of asthma, coronary heart disease, and COPD (CDC PLACES), health outcomes strongly correlated with, and often compounded by, environmental burden.

Social Vulnerability

Median household income, poverty rate, percent non-white population, and percent limited-English-speaking households (Census ACS), a proxy for a community's capacity to adapt to or recover from environmental and health stress.

Climate Exposure

Percent tree canopy coverage, derived from Philadelphia's 2018 land cover classification. Inverted so that lower canopy, and therefore higher heat exposure, corresponds with a higher risk score.

Key Findings

Where risk concentrates

The composite index identifies several tracts in Kensington and the surrounding North Philadelphia area as facing the highest combined environmental, health, and social vulnerability in the city. Tracts 176.01, 176.02, 177.02, 195.01, 195.02, 197, and 199 form a contiguous cluster scoring in the 77–81 range, consistent with the area's documented history of industrial land use and long-standing socioeconomic disinvestment.

The concentration of high scores in a single geographic cluster, rather than scattered across unrelated tracts citywide, is itself a meaningful finding: it suggests overlapping environmental and social risk factors compounding within a specific area, rather than each sub-index driving risk independently in different neighborhoods.

Two additional tracts, 9800.03 and 9807.02, scored highly (78.99 and 78.35) but fall within Philadelphia's non-standard 9800-series geography, largely Fairmount Park and similar low-population parkland. With very few residents, small raw counts there translate into statistically extreme percentile scores that don't reflect neighborhood-level vulnerability. They're included in the full dataset for completeness but excluded from this interpretation.
Methodology

How the index was built

  • Geography: Philadelphia County, at the census tract level (408 tracts), using Census TIGER/Line boundaries.
  • Normalization: every raw indicator is converted to a 0–100 percentile rank within Philadelphia, the same approach EPA's EJScreen uses, rather than comparing raw units directly against each other.
  • Weighting: the four sub-indices are combined with equal weight into the final composite score. This is a simple, defensible default rather than an attempt to justify unequal weighting.
  • EJScreen data source: EPA discontinued official public access to EJScreen in February 2025. Data used here (v2.3) is preserved and hosted by the University of Chicago's Federal Data Rescue Project, a volunteer effort to maintain access to removed federal datasets.
  • Land cover: tree canopy percentage derived via zonal histogram analysis on the University of Vermont Spatial Analysis Laboratory's 2018 Philadelphia land cover raster (7-class classification, sourced from LiDAR and NAIP imagery).
  • Code: all data processing and index calculation is available on GitHub.
